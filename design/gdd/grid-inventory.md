## Summary

Grid Inventory & Containers is where every piece of mail and every material lives between spawning and delivery: the player's hotbar, inventory and backpack, vehicle cargo, the Post Office Intake and Depot, chests, depots, the sorter's buffer, death bags and the cursor, plus items lying in the world. It gives players the "Case Your Route" moment — one Sort turns a jumbled bag into the route in order and makes room for the parcel that just bounced — and the honest co-op scrum where two friends grab the same stack and the loser visibly loses. Every automation construct reaches mail through the same interface a hand does, so it is the foundation under delivery, throwing, belts and vehicles alike, and the place where "never duplicated, never lost" is enforced.

> **Quick reference** — Layer: `Core` · Priority: `MVP (minimal M-1a → complete M1)` · Key deps: `Simulation Core, Content & Data Definitions, Networking & Session (M0+)`

## Overview

Grid Inventory & Containers is the system every piece of mail and every material lives in from the moment it spawns until it is delivered, consumed or thrown. It defines the **containers** — the player's own panel (hotbar, base inventory, backpack), vehicle cargo (bike, mail truck), the Post Office's Intake and Depot, player-built chests and depots, the Address Sorter's internal buffer, the death bag, and the one-slot **cursor** — and the rules that govern what fits where: items occupy `W×H` rectangles on a cell grid, may be rotated 90°, and stack only when their identity matches (mail by kind *and* address; materials by item type). Everything an automation construct does to mail passes through this system's interface (`TryInsert` / `TryExtract` / `TryMove`), so belts, inserters and NPC drivers are consumers of the same rules a player follows by hand.

For the player it is the thing they touch most after walking: pick mail from the Intake, see it stack and compress when addresses match, sort a truck with one click, hold a stack on the hotbar to enter delivery stance, throw it. It is also the shared surface of co-op — eight players can have the same chest open, every move is server-validated and applied in arrival order, and a losing grab rolls back visibly. Without it there is no "mail" to flow (Pillar 1), no manual path to beat a machine with (Pillar 3), and no honest contention between friends (Pillar 5).

*Design boundary:* this GDD specifies behaviour — grids, stacking, ops, validation, timers. Storage layout, allocation strategy and the replication adapter are implementation (→ become ADRs). Notation: **`W×H` = columns × rows** (Detailed Design).

## Player Fantasy

*Framing: both — the player engages directly (drag, rotate, sort, grab) and relies on the infrastructure beneath it (stacking, validated ops, shared containers). Shaped with creative-director; framing A "Case Your Route" chosen as the spine, with the chest scrum as its Fellowship beat and "faster than the sorter" as the Pillar 3 test.*

Every carrier starts the shift the same way: casing the route. You take every stack for the north side out of the Intake in one press, and the bag fills in the order the mail came off the pile — districts interleaved, a Large wedged across the middle. You hit **Sort: street** and the grid snaps into a gradient of district colours in route order: you can read your route off the panel before you read a single address. At the first house you hold the prompt once and every stack for that door leaves your bag. Two stops later a Medium won't go in — "Make space — Sort?" — you hit **Sort: size**, the bag closes its gaps, and it fits. The carrier takes this completely seriously. The bag is their pride.

*What Sort earns (re-anchored 2026-10-16, CD D-A).* **Legibility** everywhere: Sort by address puts any grid in route order (district, then street), so the next door's stacks are where the eye goes. **Space** in every grid: Sort by size defragments — when a pickup bounces `no_room` and a dry-run of Sort by size shows one Sort would make it fit, the toast offers "Make space — Sort?", and that Sort makes the same pickup succeed (H-FX-4; area alone never promises it). **Compaction** (loose singles collapsing into fat stacks) is real only where mail sits unmerged: the Intake (every insertion NoMerge, R14) and truck loads built by drag-placing (R4); the Intake is a manifest (R18), so its compaction reads as the list shortening. A bag filled by `take_all` or quick-move is merged on arrival (R8), and loose singles are never manufactured to make Sort look busier — 72 loose letters cannot fit 32 cells, and the mail must flow (Pillar 1).

Then three friends crowd the same rear door. Two hands reach for the Elm Street stack, one wins, and the other's ghost springs back into the chest with the winner's name on the toast; a take that came up short says what it got and who took the rest ("Got 4 of 10 — Jules took 6"). The grid is a shared workbench, and every grab is honest: nothing is ever duplicated, nothing silently vanishes, and the loser always sees that they lost, and to whom (art bible principle 2 — a bad outcome is never softened).

When the sorter jams mid-rush, the inventory is how a player beats the machine: you stand at the Intake, take every stack for the north side in one press, sort by hand, and load the truck yourself.

**Reference feelings.** The *space-making snap* of Tetris (the gaps close and the awkward piece fits) — taken; mandatory hand-packing — rejected, the Sort button exists so packing is never tedium. The *fondness for objects* of the Resident Evil 4 attaché case (every parcel is a toy with a shape and a glyph) — taken; paused, deliberate arranging — rejected, this is real-time co-op and the panel must work in seconds. Satisfactory's "chaos visibly becoming order" — taken at hand scale: a sorted grid is a small manifold.

**Pillars served.** Pillar 1 (*the mail must flow*): the inventory is where flow is visible. Pillar 3 (*hands beat machines, until they don't*): Sort is intentionally strong. The user ratified (2026-10-16, Open Question 17) the narrow reading now in `design/gdd/game-pillars.md`: a manual action may be instant if the machine's value is that it works unattended, and automation must never remove the player's reason to move. Belt Logistics is designed on that wording. The response to "Sort makes the Address Sorter pointless" (signal: Open Question 16) is to raise the cost of being present, never to weaken Sort — and that cost must not fall on solo players (Pillar 5). Pillar 5 (*scales 1 to 8*): the scrum is a spice, not the spine, so solo players get the full "Case Your Route" fantasy on their own; the Intake's pressure (D2) must be stated per player count, not only for a full lobby.

**Fantasy test:** (1) Sort earns its press — a sorted **grid** reads as a route at a glance (district gradient in route order, at most one non-full stack per StackKey), and a pickup offered "Make space — Sort?" succeeds after that one Sort (H-FX-4); (2) a 10-address hand route costs no more than one `open`, one `take_all` and one hold per house — ≤ 12 inputs, with no Sort, hotbar staging or open panel needed to deliver (H-FX-1); (3) a contested or partial grab is never ambiguous — the loser sees the snap-back and the winner's name, and a short take states requested vs got and who took the rest (H-FX-2, H-FX-3). Failing any of the three means the inventory has broken the fantasy.

`WorldItem` is **not** a container: it is a world entity holding exactly one entry (R12).

ContainerDef gains `layout: grid|slots|pile`, `handCarried: bool`, `acceptsInsert: bool` (default false since 2026-10-21, owner ruling — content spells it out; false = output-only: every insertion path → `not_allowed`; bags, and `po_intake` for belts/inserters via `outfeed_only`), `reservedSlots` (hotbar `[0]`) and `view` (**required** since 2026-10-21, owner ruling — no default; `auto` = D7 decides; the View column is the 100 % text default); ContainerState gains `grid` and `layout` (perks change instance dims, so dims replicate); PlayerState.containers gains `cursor`; `beltAccess` gains `inserter_only` (Content & Data CD10). Stats read (Content & Data CD11): `InventoryRows` (clamp 1…`inventory.maxRows` = **16**; Bulk Depot applies to chest and depot only), `MailMaxStack` (effective cap, replaces `stack_mult`), `WeightPenaltyPm` (`strongBackPermille` = 1000 − it). If `InventoryRows` shrinks, nothing is evicted: the grid keeps its old rows until the cut rows are empty, then shrinks (v1 perks only grow).

**R2 — Entries.** An entry occupies a `w×h` rectangle at top-left `(x,y)`, optionally `rotated` (footprint becomes `h×w`; rotation is a property of the placement, not the item; square footprints canonicalise to `rotated=false`). An entry holds either **mail** — `(mailKind, addressId, flags, mailIds[])`, `mailIds` stored ascending — or an **item** — `(itemId, count)`. `entryId` is a uint32 allocated by this system, unique per run, never reused; an entry keeps its id across container moves and across `sort` **as long as its contents (`mailIds` / `count`) are unchanged** — a `sort` consolidation that changes an entry's contents retires the old id and mints a new one (D3 step 1), so an op computed against the old contents fails `stale_entry` instead of acting on different mail. Invariant: `1 ≤ count ≤ effMax`; a 0-count entry is removed in the same op. Intake entries also carry `arrivalSeq` (R14). Widths: `entryId`, `version`, `lastReplaceVersion`, `baseVersion` and `arrivalSeq` are uint32 (never wrap within a run); `count` is uint8.

**R3 — Stacking.** `StackKey = (mailKind, addressId, flags)` for mail — Cursed is in the key, spawn shift is not, so cross-shift leftovers still compress; `StackKey = itemId` for items. `effMax = Read(MailMaxStack, kind)` (= `maxStack × stackMult`; items: `maxStack`) evaluated at op time (Bulk Mailer doubles postcard/letter); ContentValidator errors if any worst case > 255 (CV-GRD-01). Two **distinct** entries may merge only if their StackKeys match, `effMax > 1`, and the target container's `allowedCategories` admits them. Payment and penalties are always computed **per mailId** from the server's mail table, never from the stack; the wire `value` is a display hint only (→ Mail & Destinations owns rounding).

**R4 — Placement validity.** A placement is valid iff every covered cell is inside the grid and either free or covered by exactly one mergeable stack whose placement the footprint matches exactly (then the op is an implicit merge into that stack, R7; a partial overlap with a mergeable stack is `blocked`). Overlap with any non-mergeable entry, or with two or more entries → `blocked`. A stack never merges into itself: placing an entry back on its own cells with the same rotation is a no-op (`ok:true`, no version bump, no event). **Reason precedence is fixed: `out_of_bounds` is evaluated before `blocked`** — a footprint that leaves the grid is `out_of_bounds` whatever it overlaps. A drag-place onto a free cell never merges, even if a mergeable stack exists elsewhere in the container (merging is the player's choice or AutoPlace's job). **Swap: grids — no swap in v1 [D]; slots (hotbar) — a `move` from the **cursor** onto an occupied, non-mergeable slot swaps: the slot takes the cursor entry and the displaced entry goes to the cursor [D]** (one op, one version bump; the only way to restage a full hotbar without a free cell); onto a **full same-StackKey** slot → `stack_full`, no swap. A **whole** in-container move treats the mover's own old cells as free; a split-and-place (`count < total`) does not — the remainder still occupies them. Cross-container moves are all-or-nothing: compute the destination fit first, then remove and insert together.

**R5 — Insertion whitelist.** `allowedCategories` is a closed set = ItemDef categories `{tool, material, consumable, ammo, blueprint, weapon}` ∪ `{mail}`; it is checked on **every** insertion path (move, quick_move, auto-place, inserter, belt, spawn, return) and never retroactively. Rejection reason `not_allowed` ("Won't fit here" is reserved for destination-kind rejections in Mail & Destinations). `handCarried` containers (hotbar, inventory, backpack, cursor) reject `carryByHand:false` or weightClass `bulk` (`not_carryable`; dormant in v1 — no Cargo).

**R6 — Oldest-first.** Every "take k units" — split, partial move, merge overflow, quick_move, deliver, consume — removes the **k lowest mailIds**; the destination list is the sorted union. Players therefore carry and deliver the oldest mail first.

**R7 — Ops.** All ops (player and `system` requests) are server-validated and applied atomically in one FIFO (the host's in-process client included, through the same loopback adapter — Network section); automation (inserters, belts, NPC drivers) calls the same rules synchronously in its own later phase (Simulation Core R9), so a player op beats an inserter on the same tick and the inserter retries next tick. Range-gated ops (`open`, `pickup`, `deliver_matching`) carry the position the client claimed at press (Player Character P13/F5); a mutating op bumps the container's `version` by 1 (both containers for a cross-container op; a batch op — `take_all`, `deliver_matching` — bumps each touched container once); a rejected op changes no version and returns `ok:false, reason` to the requester only. Every reply carries `resultVersion` **per touched container**. **Every container event carries `actorId`** — the player id, `construct:<entityId>` for an inserter, belt or NPC driver, or `system` for spawn, expiry and drain — and a rejection or shortfall caused by another actor (`stale_entry`, `container_gone`, `no_room` lost to an inserter, a partial take) carries `byActorId` and `cause ∈ {taken, sorted, consumed, expired, destroyed, system}`, so the loser's UI can always say who won. **Partial results are explicit:** replies to `take_all`, `fromGroup` ops and `deliver_matching` carry `requested` and `got` (entries, units) plus the `byActorId`/`cause` of the largest shortfall — never a silent `ok:true` with a smaller count. A destroyed or deconstructed container, or a picked-up/expired WorldItem, leaves a **tombstone** `(id, byActorId, cause, worldSeq?)` in one bounded table (256 entries, 30 s), so a later op still gets `container_gone` naming who. **Positional ops carry `baseVersion`** (of the destination container): the server keeps `lastReplaceVersion` per container (set by `sort`'s `replace` event) and rejects a positional `move` with `stale_layout` iff `baseVersion < lastReplaceVersion` — so an op computed against a pre-sort layout can never *succeed* at an unintended spot. Ops that name only entries (`merge`, `split`, `quick_move`, `drop`, `throw`, `deliver`) need no `baseVersion`: a sort that changed that entry's contents retired its id (R2), so they fail `stale_entry`; one that only moved it is harmless to them. The requester's **own** pending sort is handled client-side (Network section: input on that container is held until the sort reply), so a player never has their own sort-then-move rejected.

Reason codes: `bad_request, not_open, out_of_range, position_stale, stale_entry, stale_layout, cursor_busy, cursor_empty, blocked, out_of_bounds, not_allowed, not_carryable, swimming, stack_full, no_room, bad_count, unsplittable, reserved_slot, sort_failed, container_gone, no_target, wallet_floor`; range-gated requests (open, pickup, deliver) first pass Player Character P13 (`dead → frozen → no_target → unavailable → out_of_range / position_stale`), and `throw` adds Throwing's codes (above). Simulation Core's reserved `busy` (op ring full) may answer any op. `bad_request` is returned for any malformed op (unknown op, a `count` < 0 or > 255, coordinates outside int16, an id never allocated — at or above the allocator's high-water and outside the connection's temp namespace; a retired, consumed or failed-temp id is `stale_entry` —, `rotated` on a slots layout, `throw` naming a non-hotbar entry) and never crashes the server; a well-formed `count` that is wrong for the entry's *current* size (0, or ≥ total for `split`, > total for `move`) is `bad_count` — reachable when a stack shrinks under an open stepper. Reason precedence for `split` is fixed: `bad_request → not_open → stale_entry → cursor_busy → unsplittable → bad_count`; for any placement: `bad_request → not_open → stale_entry → stale_layout → reserved_slot → out_of_bounds → not_carryable → not_allowed → blocked / stack_full`. **R8 — AutoPlace (shared by quick_move, spawn, return, inserter, belt, pickup, catch, grant).**
```
AutoPlace(entry e, targets T[], mergePolicy):
  PASS M (merge)     : for each t in T, for each stack s of t in row-major order (y asc, then x asc)
                       where CanMerge(e, s) and s.count < effMax: take min(units, effMax − s.count) oldest ids.
                       Skipped when mergePolicy == NoMerge or effMax == 1.
  PASS U (unrotated) : for each t in T: scan y = 0..H−1 (outer), x = 0..W−1 (inner), top-left anchor;
                       first position where the w×h footprint is in bounds and free. Slots layout: lowest free usable slot ≥ 1.
  PASS R (rotated)   : only if w ≠ h; same scan with footprint h×w, rotated = true.
  Targets that fail allowedCategories are skipped in all passes.
  Nothing moved: if no target admitted the entry's category → not_allowed; else → no_room.
  Something moved → ok (partial allowed for stackables).
```
By default (`order = PassFirst`) all three passes run over **all** targets before the next pass starts, so rotation is a last resort across the whole target list. `order = TargetFirst` (mail catch and WorldItem pickup only, R9) runs M → U → R on each target before trying the next, so the hotbar is filled before any inventory merge. Row-major order has no ties (two entries never share a top-left). The merge pass's lookup of non-full stacks by StackKey is amortised O(1) (index → ADR).

Caught and picked-up mail lands on the hotbar so the catch-and-throw relay works without opening the panel.

**R10 — Open set, range, trust.** A player's open set is their own panel + cursor + at most one **external** container (chest, depot, bike or truck cargo, sorter console, a bag, the Intake or PO Depot). Opening another closes the first. `open` requires ≤ 2.5 m from the container's **interaction anchor** (a construct's tile centre; a building container's console — the Intake and PO Depot consoles are the anchors, not the building) or being mounted in the vehicle (mounting makes the cargo the player's open external — subscribed and leashed — but never opens the panel or enters Panel context; mounting or entering Build while Holding first auto-`return`s — "Stowed" — and `no_room` refuses the mount, Input & Controls rule 6), else `out_of_range` (spec 03 §3.4); the server **auto-closes at > 3.0 m** from the same anchor (ch06's figure; the leash knob's floor is 3.0 m because Player Character's validator rule 1 needs range + 0.5 m slack ≤ leash, so an open never succeeds outside the leash). The client fades the external panel's edge from 2.5 m and shows "walking away" so the close is never a surprise; on close the panel slides out and a toast says "Out of reach"; on a **leash auto-close** a cursor entry is put away into the panel (R9 cursor row; it stays on the cursor only if the panel is full), so walking off mid-drag never leaves a player Holding into a raid. Range is Player Character **F5** (movement is client-authoritative, spec 13 §3): the chest point of the position the client **claimed at press** must lie within range + 0.5 m of the anchor, corroborated within 1.5 m by a reported or dead-reckoned sample from the last 300 ms; no transform for 1 s → `position_stale`; the leash uses the newest sample and never fires on a stale position — a consistency rule, not anti-cheat. Per-op range checks and rate limits are dropped (ch13 §3, §4.3); the open-set check is kept as a *consistency* rule, not anti-cheat. Panel containers are never in another player's open set; external containers may be open to many players at once. Ownership checks on constructs do not exist (§4.3): anyone may open any chest, depot or bag.

**R11 — Cursor.** Server-side one-entry container per player; other players never see it. Rotation while holding is client-only (the next op carries `rotated`). The cursor survives a manual close of the external container or the panel (leash auto-close puts it away, R10) (the HUD shows a held-entry chip beside the hotbar until it is placed, returned or put away). On **death** its entry goes into the bag; on **disconnect** it is AutoPlaced into the panel, else spawned as a WorldItem (`origin = Dropped`) at the body **[D]**. The frozen body then follows ch06 §6 and is **not openable** in v1 (Player Character P19 — the one owner-locked inventory; OQ26): reconnect within 120 s resumes with the panel intact (Network section); otherwise **the body spills a bag exactly like death (R13)** — this closes former Open Question 3, which spec 06 §6 already answers.

**R12 — WorldItem.** Fields: `worldItemId` (EntityId class `world_item`), `entries[]` (**exactly one entry, except `origin = Spill`, which holds every non-mail entry of the bag it came from — one heap, one WorldItem**), `position` (`Vec3Mm`, int32 mm, per Simulation Core numerics / ADR-0006 §1; wire width per the network ADR), `spawnTick`, `expiresTick`, `origin ∈ {Dropped, Thrown, BeltLeak, Spill, Loot}`, `ownerId` and `tint` (set for Spill; the owner's tint strap and hat icon, R13 / CD condition). No auto-merge between WorldItems. Pickup (`pickup` op, ≤ 2.5 m) = AutoPlace one entry into the panel by R9 (mail hotbar-first; partial allowed; `no_room` if nothing fits); a heap survives with its remaining entries; two simultaneous pickups of the same entry: first arrival wins, second gets `container_gone` with `byActorId`. A thrown item's timer starts at **landing**. **Water:** an item landing on a non-ground tile does not vanish — it floats for `waterReturnSeconds = 10` **PROPOSED** from the landing tick (visible gag; no pickup prompt is offered), then mail returns to the Intake **at the back of the outfeed queue** (R14 arrival order; the mailId and deadline are unchanged, so nothing is softened and nothing jumps the queue) and non-mail is destroyed. **Expiry: mail → Intake (or backlog if full), also at the back of the queue; non-mail → destroyed [D]** (otherwise dropping logs far away is a free teleport). **Loot mail** (`origin = Loot`, Mail & Destinations R15) never expires and is never a cap candidate until Payday start; once picked up it is ordinary mail (a later drop gets the 300 s timer). **Same-tick ordering:** expiry and cap eviction run after `Inputs.Apply`, so a pickup that arrives on the expiry tick wins. Soft cap `worldItemCap = 512` **PROPOSED** counts WorldItems, not entries (a Spill heap is one): beyond it, evict the WorldItem with the lowest `expiresTick` (ties by lowest `worldItemId`) whose entry is **mail and older than `capEvictMinAgeSeconds = 10`**, i.e. age ≥ 300 ticks — a letter created at tick c qualifies from c + 300 (owner ruling 2026-10-21, `>=`) (lossless — it returns to the Intake; a throw that just landed is never evicted under a teammate's hand); if no mail qualifies, the non-mail WorldItem with the lowest `expiresTick` (ties lowest `worldItemId`) is destroyed with `ItemsLost`; the newly created item is never the candidate. If nothing qualifies (every WorldItem is young mail, e.g. a 400-unit belt leak), the cap is exceeded temporarily, re-checked each tick and logged (`worldItemCapExceeded`). Candidate lookup is amortised O(1) (per-class expiry FIFOs → ADR). **Wallet floor on a thrown stack:** if a wrong-mailbox landing stops at `wallet_floor`, the unconsumed remainder becomes a WorldItem (`origin = Thrown`) at the mailbox — FLIGHT always ends in exactly one location state (R19). **Payday start** (Mail & Destinations R11, R15), in order: every unit with L ≥ `deadLetterShifts` in any container, cursor, bag, WorldItem or the backlog is removed as **returned to sender** (CONSUMED, `actorId = system`, no penalty — the dead-letter sink); then each unclaimed Loot WorldItem returns to the back of the Intake queue (or backlog), ascending `worldItemId`.

**R13 — Death bag and spill bags.** On death, a **pile** bag at the death location (at swim depth: the nearest walkable dry point within 6 m, else the water surface — Player Character P15; a bag or Spill heap on water floats in place on its normal timer, since R12's water rule covers landings only, and Open on a bag / `pickup` on a heap are allowed while swimming) receives 1:1 the entries of the hotbar (unless Second Wind keeps it), inventory, backpack and cursor; the equipped backpack *item* stays equipped, its contents drop **[D]**. A destroyed vehicle, destroyed chest/depot, or **deconstructing a non-empty container** spills a bag the same way. Bags are output-only. Expiry (`deathBagDespawnSeconds`, 30): mail → Intake (or backlog), at the back of the outfeed queue; **non-mail becomes one `Spill` WorldItem (a heap holding every non-mail entry) at the bag's position [D]** (300 s, then destroyed per R12; it carries the bag owner's `ownerId`/`tint` so teammates can still find "Sam's pile" — CD-GDD-ALIGN condition; one heap is one WorldItem against the cap, so a wipe of eight players is eight WorldItems, not 448) — never the PO Depot, which would make death a free materials teleport; the cost is time and distance, never position, so ch01's "no permanent penalty" survives for anyone who walks back. Bags have **no owner lock and no owner grace** (R10: no ownership checks; CD D-E — an owner-only window protected nobody, since the owner is still respawning, and contradicted the anti-pillars); findability is the owner's tint strap, hat icon and name ("Sam's pile"). An empty spill (B = 0) creates no bag. The Post Office reaching 0 HP is Run Over (spec 01 §end conditions), so the Intake and PO Depot never spill.

**R14 — Spawn into the Intake.** `Spawn(container, units, mergePolicy)`: the mail spawner passes **NoMerge [D]** — one unit per entry, first-fit placement — so the Intake is an unsorted pile and the Sort button means something (spec 03 §1.2). **Every insertion into the Intake is NoMerge** — spawn, return-on-expiry, water return, bag expiry and the backlog drain alike: it never merges into a resident stack. Spawns are one unit per entry; a **returned entry enters whole** (a returned 20-stack is one 20-unit entry, not 20 singles — NoMerge means "no merge", not "split"). Every automatic add into any *other* container merges. **Outfeed order = ascending entry `arrivalSeq`** (uint32, a per-Intake counter stamped on each **entry** as it is inserted; fresh spawns therefore leave in mailId order, returned mail leaves after everything already there — a missed throw or a water landing can never jump the queue). An Intake `sort` that consolidates entries gives the new entry the **maximum** `arrivalSeq` of its parts — no entry's `arrivalSeq` ever decreases, so consolidated mail leaves no earlier than its latest-arrived part (its older parts may leave later than unsorted — the cost noted in OQ15); within an entry R6 applies (`TakeUnit` takes its lowest mailId). There is no per-unit side array. `PeekOutfeed` returns the lowest mailId of the entry with the lowest `arrivalSeq`, amortised O(1) (a per-container head index maintained on insert/take, not a scan — H-PERF-6 gates the probe count; FIFO ring vs heap → ADR); Presort swaps the selector to street grouping, one head index per street — Belt Logistics owns selector policy, this system exposes `TakeUnit(container, selector)`. On any other container the `FIFO` selector means the container's lowest mailId (R6). When the Intake is full — **or while the backlog is non-empty** — spawned and returned mail **appends** to the **backlog** FIFO owned by Mail & Destinations without probing the Intake (nothing overtakes the backlog). **The drain** runs each tick after `Inputs.Apply` and exits in O(1) when the backlog is empty or the Intake has no free cell; otherwise it runs only if the Intake's `version` was changed by anything other than the drain itself, the backlog grew, or the last pass hit `backlogDrainPerTick`. It skips any entry whose footprint area exceeds the Intake's free-cell count, uses D3's per-shape monotone start hints within one pass (occupancy only grows during a pass), places at most `backlogDrainPerTick = 32` **PROPOSED** entries per tick, and scans past a Large that does not fit (no head-of-line blocking). The backlog itself is unbounded by design (nothing is lost) but its length is a telemetry metric and OQ15's instrument.

**R15 — Perks that change containers.** Dimension growth **appends rows at the bottom** and never moves existing coordinates; perks apply live and only grow. Bulk Depot: `rows' = ceil(rows × 3/2)` (chest 8×4→8×6; depot 16×10→16×15 = 240 cells → manifest). Existing stacks are never split when `effMax` changes.

**R16 — Weight.** Counted containers: hotbar, inventory, backpack, **cursor**. Vehicles and all external containers are not counted; a mounted rider is not weight-scaled; swimming is. **`weightBasis = PerEntry` [D]** — one `classPts` value per grid entry regardless of count (Formulas D1). Non-mail ItemDefs default to `light` **PROPOSED** (ch10 §1 lists no classes). Strong Back: clamp first, then halve the penalty **[D]**.

**R17 — Grants, build costs, salvage.** `Grant(player, entry, overflowPolicy ∈ {Reject, SpillWorld, ToDepot})`: tool/weapon/consumable target hotbar → inventory → backpack; everything else inventory → backpack → hotbar; `ToDepot` AutoPlaces the overflow into the PO Depot (then backlog-style `no_room` if even that is full). `CountItem(containers[], id)` / `ConsumeItems(containers[], id, n)` are all-or-nothing over the panel (inventory, backpack, hotbar) then the PO Depot; within a container smallest stack first, ties by later row-major position. `CountItem` reads a per-container `itemId → units` index maintained on every mutation (the build ghost may query it every frame), never a scan.

**R18 — Manifest view** (`view = manifest` by D7's cells / scaled-width / scaled-height rule; e.g. the 16-wide depot flips at ≥ 150 % text scale **[D]**): client groups the full state by `(kind, addressId/itemId, flags)`; drops onto a group are `move` / `quick_move` with a `fromGroup` key the server resolves to concrete units **oldest-first by mailId (R6) — never "partial stacks first", which would contradict oldest-first**; "any free space" is AutoPlace. Non-openers receive group counts only (the HUD manifest, Network); any op still requires `open` (R10). A manifest row also offers Take all (`take_all` with that row's address or district as the filter). The client regroups at most once per rendered frame and pools its rows; a group's optimistic display is a count decrement only. The 8×4 "page" is the bag's UI page size, not a capacity.

**R19 — Conservation invariant.** Every unit (mailId or item unit) is in exactly one location state at all times (table below). Debug builds assert the full invariant once per tick behind `[Conditional("INVENTORY_ASSERT")]` (compiled out of Release, so perf budgets are measured without it); every build keeps O(1) per-state unit counters, updated in the lowest mutation primitive, and checks `Σ counters = spawned + granted − consumed − destroyed` once per tick, logging (never crashing) on mismatch — the field check a dedicated server runs. A fuzz test asserts the multiset of mailIds and item units is conserved across random op sequences (the M0 gate's "never duplicated or lost").

## Formulas

*Specialist: systems-designer. All arithmetic is integer; speeds in mm/s and multipliers in permille. Variables marked `data file` are tuning knobs (Section G).*

### D1 — Weight → speed multiplier

The `weightSpeed` formula is defined as:

```
u(e)          = 1                                   (weightBasis = PerEntry)
P             = Σ_{e ∈ hotbar ∪ inventory ∪ backpack ∪ cursor} classPts[weightClass(e)] × u(e)
penalty0      = min(ptsPermille × P, 1000 − weightSpeedFloorPermille)
penalty       = (penalty0 × (1000 − strongBackPermille)) / 1000        (integer floor)
speedPermille = 1000 − penalty
moveSpeed     = baseSpeed_mm_s × speedPermille / 1000                   (int64; one multiplier)
moveSpeed     = baseSpeed_mm_s × speedPermille × otherPermille / 1_000_000   (int64; two multipliers — ONE division at the end, never two)
```
`baseSpeed_mm_s` is the current locomotion base: walk 5000, sprint 7500, **swim 2000** (spec 11 §8; swimming is weight-scaled, R16). `classPts[bulk]` is undefined by design — bulk is never in a counted container (R5), and ContentValidator errors if a `handCarried` container could admit it.

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| classPts[c] | int | light 1 / medium 3 / heavy 8 | data file (`weightPoints`, spec 11 §8) | points per entry by weight class; bulk is not hand-carriable |
| weightClass(e) | enum | light/medium/heavy | MailKindDef / ItemDef | class of the entry; non-mail default `light` (PROPOSED) |
| u(e) | int | 1 | constant under PerEntry | units counted (count(e) under PerUnit — not used) |
| weightBasis | enum | {PerEntry, PerUnit} | data file (PROPOSED knob, default PerEntry) | see degenerate cases |
| P | int | 0–96 (default panel: 7 hotbar + 16 + 16 entries + cursor = 40 entries, but only 12 Large footprints fit = 96 pts); 112 with Big Pockets ×2 | calculated | weight points; replicated as `PlayerState.weightPoints` (int16) |
| ptsPermille | int | 10 | data file (0.01 × 1000) | penalty per point |
| weightSpeedFloorPermille | int | 600 | data file (`weightSpeedFloor` 0.6) | floor |
| strongBackPermille | int | {0, 500} | perk (Strong Back) | halves the penalty after clamping |
| speedPermille | int | 600–1000 (800–1000 with Strong Back) | calculated | output |

**Output Range:** non-increasing, piecewise-linear; 1000 at P = 0; **floor 600 reached at P ≥ 40** (39 → 610, 40 → 600, 41 → 600); plateau above 40. With Strong Back the floor is 800.
**Examples:** one Large package: P = 8 → 920 (matches the spec's "92 %"). Four Mediums in base inventory: P = 12 → 880. Five Large: P = 40 → 600; with Strong Back → 800. A fully loaded 39-entry panel of letter stacks: P = 39 → 610 (PerUnit would give 780 points → 600: letters would dominate and hoarding past 40 points would be free — the reason PerEntry was chosen). Land-kit start (axe + fiber): P = 2 → 980.
**Degenerate:** PerUnit couples to thrown damage (a 20-letter stack would hit for 100 under ch13 §4.1's "5 × weight") — thrown damage must be defined from `classPts`, not `P` (→ Throwing & Catching). Composing walk-speed multipliers in separate integer divisions truncates twice: sprint 7500 at P = 39 **with Strong Back** (805‰) and a 1500‰ buff gives `7500 × 805 × 1500 / 1 000 000 = 9056` in one division but `(7500 × 805 / 1000) × 1500 / 1000 = 6037 × 1.5 = 9055` in two — the two forms only differ when `baseSpeed × speedPermille` is not a multiple of 1000, which is why the test uses 805 and not 610. At default knobs `penalty0` is a multiple of 10 and halving is exact, so D1 itself never truncates; an odd `ptsPermille` would.

### D2 — Stack capacity and cell usage

The `cellUsage` formula is defined as:

```
effMax(k) = maxStack(k) × stackMult(k)
E_g       = ceil(n_g / effMax) = (n_g + effMax − 1) / effMax
cells_g   = E_g × w × h
fill_g    = n_g / (E_g × effMax)
```

**Variables:**
| Variable | Type | Range | Source | Description |
|----------|------|-------|--------|-------------|
| maxStack(k) | int | postcard 40, letter 20, packages 1 | MailKindDef (spec 03 §1.1) | base stack cap |
| stackMult(k) | int | {1, 2} in v1 content; knob range 1–3 | perk (Bulk Mailer) | doubles postcard/letter |
| effMax(k) | int | 1–80 in v1 (up to 240 at the knob extremes: maxStack 80 × stackMult 3) | calculated | effective cap, evaluated at op time; `mailIds[]` and `count` are sized for 255 |
| n_g | int | ≥ 1 | state | units sharing one StackKey |
| E_g | int | 1–n_g | calculated | entries needed |
| w, h | int | 1–4 | MailKindDef grid | footprint |

**Output Range:** E_g minimal when n_g is a multiple of effMax; worst fill is `(e + 1) / (2e)` at n_g = effMax + 1 — 52.5 % at effMax 20, 51.25 % at 40; maximum n_g entries when every address is distinct.
**Examples:** 20 letters to 20 addresses = 20 cells; to one address = 1 cell. Shift-1 solo (~72 letters over 10 houses): sorted ≈ 10 cells vs unsorted 72 (7.2× compression). **Intake pressure by player count** (Mail & Destinations F3 — counts scale by `n^0.65`; NoMerge spawn; shifts 1–5 run 240/270/270/270/300 s): zero-pickup shift-5 inflow is ≈ **1,035 cells at 8 players** (the 320-cell Intake is full ≈ 79 s in), ≈ 660 at 4 (full ≈ 124 s in) and ≈ 268 solo (never full); at 8 players even shift 1 brings ≈ 373 cells. These are **upper bounds** on time-to-full: first-fit leaves 2×4 holes that singles fill but Larges cannot. Returns enter whole (R14), so a death spilling ten 20-letter stacks adds 10 Intake cells, not 200. A group that does not keep the outfeed moving meets backlog **routinely**; solo meets it only through returns — which is why OQ15's instrument counts "Sort pressed while `backlogUnits` > 0" separately per lobby size.

### D3 — Sort packing (consolidating first-fit-decreasing)

1. **Consolidate**: per StackKey, take units ascending by mailId and chunk into effMax-sized stacks (oldest first). A chunk whose `mailIds` equal an existing entry's list **keeps that entry's id**; every other chunk gets a **new** entryId and the group's old ids retire (R2) — so an op computed before the sort can never act on contents it did not see. New ids are minted in a fixed order — StackKeys ascending by (kindIndex, addressId, flags), chunks oldest first — so H-DET-1 holds. In the Intake a new chunk's `arrivalSeq` is the maximum of the entries whose units it took (R14). Consolidation only reduces entries.
2. **Order** by the comparator below (every key integer; final tiebreak entryId — the order is total and algorithm-independent, which is what makes the result reproducible in the test harness).
3. **Place** each entry with AutoPlace passes U then R, no merging.
4. **Failure path.** If any entry is unplaced: in **size** mode there is no retry (it would be the same computation) — abort `sort_failed`, container untouched. In **address** mode retry once in size order; if that places everything, apply it and reply `ok:true, appliedMode: size` (the UI says "Tidied by size — no room to sort by street"); if that fails too, abort `sort_failed`. Size-mode first-fit-decreasing on v1 footprints (1×1, 1×2, 2×2, 2×4) in even-width grids is **believed never to fail on a state reachable through ops**; this is not proven, so the abort path is mandatory and H-D3-6 tests it empirically on reachable states. Address mode *can* fail on valid packs (8×4 chest: a letter first at (0,0) pushes three Larges to x = 1, 3, 5 and leaves no 2×2 hole for a Medium, although a hand-pack exists), which is why it has the retry. Pre-check: if Σ footprint area > cells, fail without retry (a defensive guard — unreachable through ops, since every entry is already placed; testable only via a test seam).

| Mode | Comparator (ascending unless noted) |
|---|---|
| Sort by size | area `w·h` **desc** → `max(w,h)` desc → mail before items → mail: kindIndex, then (districtId, streetId, number, unit; absent unit = 0), then flags; items: itemId index → entryId |
| Sort by address | mail before items → mail: (districtId, streetId, number, unit) → area desc → `max(w,h)` desc → kindIndex, flags → entryId; items follow all mail by category rank, itemId index, area desc, entryId |

`kindIndex` and itemId index come from content ordinal order (sorted ids), not load order. "Sort by address" is **district index then street** (not hue — the Okabe–Ito district hues have no natural gradient; the art bible §7 "hue gradient" is satisfied because district index order *is* the swatch order).

**Variables:** W, H (ContainerState.grid), C = W·H, n (consolidated entries, ≤ C), a ≤ 8 (max footprint area), S ≤ 6 (distinct oriented shapes in v1).
**Runtime:** naive first-fit ≤ 2C × ΣA where ΣA = Σ footprint areas ≤ C (n entries of area a cannot all coexist — n·a ≤ C), so the Intake's real naive bound is ≈ 2 × 320 × 320 ≈ 205 k cell probes (a 4000-cell container ≈ 32 M). With **per-shape monotone start hints** — invariant: *occupancy only grows during one pack, so an anchor that failed for an oriented shape can never succeed later in the same pack; each oriented shape therefore keeps a cursor that only advances* — the bound is ≤ Σ_shapes (C × a_shape) + n × a: the six v1 oriented shapes have areas summing to ≈ 25, so the Intake ≈ 8 k probes and a 4000-cell container ≈ 100 k — identical result to naive first-fit (H-D3-5). Sort is cheap; the per-tick costs that matter are the backlog drain and outfeed (R14, H-PERF-1). Comparator cost n log n (≈ 2.7 k compares at 320).
**Output Range:** positions bounded by the grid; cannot fail when all entries are 1×1 (n ≤ C); otherwise 2D packing is NP-hard and first-fit is a heuristic — the abort path is mandatory, and a property test must assert *nothing lost*, *idempotent*, *`sort_failed` leaves version unchanged*.
**Example:** bike 8×2 with two Large packages (2×4 each): both rotate to 4×2 and pack at (0,0) and (4,0) — exact fit. Sorter buffer 4×2 holding a Large (8 cells) + a Medium (4) + four letters (4) = 16 > 8 → `sort_failed` on the area pre-check.

### D4 — Death-bag size

```
B     = E_hotbar × (1 − keepHotbar) + E_inventory + E_backpack + E_cursor
pages = ceil(B / 32)
```
**Variables:** E_hotbar 0–7, E_inventory 0–16 (32 with Big Pockets ×2), E_backpack 0–16, E_cursor 0–1 (state); keepHotbar ∈ {0,1} (perk Second Wind).
**Output Range:** B ≤ 56 for a player (2 pages); ≤ 80 for a wrecked truck (3 pages); ≤ 160 for a destroyed depot (5 pages) and ≤ 240 with Bulk Depot (8 pages); a chest ≤ 48 (2 pages). The Intake and PO Depot never spill (R13). Overflow is zero by construction (pile layout). A grid bag cannot work: a panel's footprint cells reach 96 (112 with Big Pockets) against 32, and even a full 32-cell inventory is not guaranteed to repack into 32.
**Example:** hotbar 4, inventory 9, backpack 6, cursor 1 → B = 20, one page.

### D5 — Intake backlog tick (boundary; owned by Mail & Destinations / Run Structure)

```
period = 15 s × 30 Hz = 450 ticks, metronome aligned to Delivery start, runs in Delivery and Raid only
each period: if backlogUnits > 0: complaint += backlogComplaintPerPeriod
net per period while complaint > 0 = backlogComplaintPerPeriod − complaintDecayPerSecond × 15   (= value − 1.5 at the spec's decay)
periods per shift = shiftSeconds / 15 = 16 (240 s) · 18 (270 s) · 20 (300 s); shifts 1–5 = 16+18+18+18+20 = 90 periods
```
Complaint is held in **permille** (decay 1.5/period = 1500‰, threshold 75 = 75 000‰) so all arithmetic stays integer.
**Finding:** at the spec's +1 per period the net is 1 − 1.5 = **−0.5** — backlog never raises the meter on its own. The knob was named "per tick" in spec 01; it fires once per 450 sim ticks, so this GDD calls it **`backlogComplaintPerPeriod`** and asks Run Structure to adopt the name. **PROPOSED value 3**, derived from a target rather than the sign alone: *a backlog ignored for two full shifts should put the team one shift from the Inspector* — net +1.5/period gives +24 in shift 1 (240 s, 16 periods) and +27 in shift 2 (270 s), **+51** after the first two shifts against the 75 threshold, Inspector during the third. Value 2 (net +0.5) reaches only 45 over all 90 periods and never bites; value 4 (net +2.5) reaches 75 inside two shifts. For the knob to bite at all within five shifts the net must be ≥ 75 / 90 ≈ 0.84, so the safe range relative to decay is `decay × 15 + 0.85 … decay × 15 + 2.5` (2.35–4 at the spec's decay). Decay outside Delivery/Raid is Run Structure's and not modelled here. The increment is flat per period regardless of backlog size by design — the signal is "the Intake is blocked", not "how badly". Owner: Run Structure — flagged in Open Questions, not decided here, and **not to be settled until OQ15's instrument has data**. "Intake full" in ch01/ch11 should read `backlogUnits > 0` (an exactly-full Intake has no backlog). The backlog carries across shifts and is never lost.

### D6 — Despawn timers

```
expiresTick       = createdTick + seconds × 30
deathBagTicks     = deathBagDespawnSeconds × 30   = 900  (30 s)
worldItemTicks    = worldItemDespawnSeconds × 30  = 9000 (300 s)
retrievalWindow_s = max(0, deathBagDespawnSeconds − respawnSeconds)
retrievalRadius_m = speed_m_s × retrievalWindow_s        (path distance from the respawn point, not the death point)
waterReturnTicks  = waterReturnSeconds × 30 = 300 (10 s)
```
**Variables:** `deathBagDespawnSeconds` 30 (data file; safe 20–120, hard floor > respawnSeconds), `worldItemDespawnSeconds` 300 (data file; safe 60–600), `respawnSeconds` 10 (3 with Second Wind), speed walk 5 / sprint 7.5 / bike 8 m/s (spec 11 §8, 10 §1.3), `createdTick` = landing tick for thrown items.
**Output Range / Examples:** retrieval radius at 30 s / 10 s: window 20 s → 100 m walking, 150 m sprinting, 160 m by bike; with Second Wind 27 s → 135 m. Timers are absolute sim ticks (freeze in a solo pause; run through Prep/Payday/Draft).
**Degenerate:** `deathBagDespawnSeconds ≤ respawnSeconds` → window clamps to 0, the owner can never reach the bag (ContentValidator warning names both keys); `= 0` → mail goes straight to the Intake and non-mail to a Spill heap; `worldItemDespawnSeconds < ~30` → thrown mail vanishes before a teammate can catch or fetch it; `capEvictMinAgeSeconds ≥ worldItemDespawnSeconds` → nothing is ever evictable and the cap is only enforced by expiry (validator warning).

### D7 — Small formulas
- `version += 1` per mutating op per container.
- Manifest threshold: `view = manifest` iff `cells > manifestCellThreshold (160)` **or** `W × cellPx × textScale > panelInnerWidthPx` **or** `H × cellPx × textScale > panelInnerHeightPx` (`cellPx = 56`; both panel dimensions from the UX spec; the height clause is what flips an 8×10 truck at 150 % text scale on 1080p — 840 px tall). `textScale` is a **game-owned** settings scalar that multiplies cell size and font size together; the view mode is computed from the measured panel size, not from a guessed window size.
- Bulk Depot rows: `rows' = (rows × 3 + 1) / 2` (integer).
- Pending display: an optimistic op renders as confirmed until `pendingDebounceMs = 250` **PROPOSED** has passed without a reply, then as dashed-pending (so a 60–120 ms home connection never flickers); the ≤ 150 ms confirm figure in Game Feel is the *target RTT + 1 tick*, not a UI deadline.

## Edge Cases

*Specialist: systems-designer, from the rule and formula space of Sections C and D.*

### Zero, max and out-of-range
- **If a `move` carries `count` equal to the entry's total**: it is a plain move — the entry keeps its `entryId`, no split, one version bump per touched container. `count = 0` or `count > total` → `bad_count`; `count < 0` or `> 255` → `bad_request` (R7); nothing changes.
  *(Errata note 2026-10-22, pending Evan — a Mail Lead planning default, not a ruling: story 007 follows Accepted ADR-0009 §1, so `count = 0` is a whole-entry move like `count == total`; only `count > total` → `bad_count` (and `1 ≤ count < total` until story 008's split-and-place). The rule text here and in R7 is unchanged until Evan rules; TD-150, TD-105 item 3.)*
- **If `split` targets an entry with `effMax = 1`** (any package or tool): `unsplittable`, whatever `k` was sent; the fixed reason precedence (`not_open → stale_entry → cursor_busy → unsplittable → bad_count`) means the same bad request always returns the same code.
- **If the panel and cursor are empty (`P = 0`)**: `speedPermille = 1000` — exactly `baseSpeed`; the division by 1000 is exact.
- **If `P ≥ 40`**: `penalty0` clamps at 400 → 600; P = 41, 60 and the theoretical 112 all give 600 (800 with Strong Back). Weight past the floor is free; accepted, because PerEntry bounds hoarding by entry count.
- **If `sort` runs on an empty container, or the packed result equals the current layout**: `ok:true`, no `replace` event, **no version bump** — idempotent sorts are free so sort-spam costs only the pack computation.
- **If a death, wreck or deconstruct would spill `B = 0` entries**: no bag entity is created.
- **If a ContainerDef loads with `W < 1`, `H < 1`, or any `maxStack < 1` / `stackMult < 1`**: ContentValidator fails the load (error, not warning); the run does not start. R2's invariant cannot hold for `effMax = 0`, so this is never a runtime case.
- **If a client sends `rotated=true` for a non-square entry whose `h×w` footprint leaves the grid**: `out_of_bounds`; the server never infers "they meant unrotated".
- **If a placement both leaves the grid and overlaps an entry** (a Large at (5,1) in an 8×4 chest with a Medium at (6,2)): `out_of_bounds` — bounds are checked before overlap (R4 precedence), so the same bad request always returns the same code.
- **If the deliver hold finds no match and the selected slot holds no mail** (hands, a tool): `no_target`, nothing changes, the prompt shows "Nothing for this address". If `take_all`'s filter matches nothing: `no_target`.
- **If `take_all` meets an entry that does not fit** (a Large with only singles' holes free): that entry is skipped, the batch continues, and the reply lists it under `skipped` — the toast reads "Took 9 of 10 — 1 didn't fit".
- **If a malformed op arrives** (count −1, x = 70000, an id that was never allocated, `rotated` on the hotbar): `bad_request`, nothing changes, no event; a fuzz lane sends random bytes and asserts the server never throws.

### Simultaneous ops
- **If two players `pick` the same whole entry in one tick**: FIFO decides; the second gets `stale_entry` with `byActorId` = the winner, and their ghost springs back under a toast naming the winner (the loser always sees that they lost, and to whom). If both instead `split` k units from a 20-stack and `2k ≤ 20`, **both succeed** — the first takes the k lowest mailIds, the second the next k; contention is on entries, not on a stack's contents.
- **If a positional `move` and another player's `sort` on the same container arrive in one tick**: FIFO. Sort-then-move: the move carries a `baseVersion` older than `lastReplaceVersion` → `stale_layout`; it can never land at a position the player did not see. **If the sort was the mover's own**: the client holds input on that container until the sort reply (Network section), so the move is sent with the new `baseVersion` and succeeds; if a foreign sort coalesced into the same `replace`, the held move is shown as a snap-back instead.
- **If a `split` or `quick_move` names an entry that another player's `sort` consolidated**: the entry's id was retired (D3 step 1) → `stale_entry`; the player's view refreshes from the `replace` event and they pick again. An entry the sort only moved keeps its id and the op succeeds — its contents are what the player saw.
- **If an inserter's `TryInsert` and a player's `quick_move` both target the last free cell**: on the same tick the player wins (ops apply in phase 0, automation in phase 5 — Simulation Core R9); the inserter's call fails, the unit stays on the arm and Belt Logistics retries next tick. A player loses only to an inserter that filled the cell on an earlier tick: `no_room` with `byActorId = construct:<inserter>`, toast "The inserter got it first". Never a partial insert.
- **If a catcher's panel has no room for caught mail**: fumble — the stack bounces to a WorldItem at the catcher's feet (R9, CD D-C); never the cursor, never softened (Pillar 2). **Room for part of it**: the oldest units go in and the newest bounce (`unitsIn 15, unitsFumbled 5`).
- **If two players pick up the same WorldItem**: first arrival takes it; second gets `container_gone` with `byActorId`. If the first pickup was partial (only part fits their panel), the WorldItem survives with the remainder and the second pickup succeeds against what is left — no duplicate. A pickup that arrives on the item's expiry or eviction tick wins (R12 ordering).
- **If a `merge` names the same entry as source and target, or a `move` places an entry on its own cells with the same rotation**: merge → `blocked`; move → no-op `ok:true`, no version bump, no event.
- **If a split-and-place (`count < total`) targets cells overlapping the source's own footprint**: `blocked` — the remainder still occupies them (R4 as corrected).

### Rule collisions
- **If a Misdeliver is held on a 20-letter stack**: exactly **one** unit (the oldest) is consumed, penalised and counted against the complaint meter; the other 19 stay held and the prompt still shows ✗. Holding again misdelivers the next. A careless hold therefore costs one letter, not a full meter (OQ4, decided). A **thrown** 20-stack landing in the wrong box is the opposite case by the user's decision: all 20 units misdeliver — 20 penalties (money is never capped); complaint per Mail & Destinations R9 (door bucket, ≤ 20 per op, then the team bucket) — stopping only at the wallet floor, where the unconsumed remainder becomes a WorldItem at the mailbox (R12) — a bad throw is never softened (Pillar 2); Throwing & Catching owns that consume loop and calls `Consume` per unit here. The unit is consumed only if `wallet − penalty(mailId) ≥ −500` (exactly −500 allowed); otherwise `wallet_floor` and nothing changes. Correct deliveries consume the whole entry and never stop early.
- **If the deliver hold runs at a mailbox with three matching stacks and one Cursed stack for the same address**: `deliver_matching` delivers all four (Cursed is a flag on the StackKey, not an address mismatch; whether the mailbox *accepts* Cursed is Mail & Destinations' rule and is evaluated per entry); a stack for the house next door is never touched.
- **If a Cursed and a non-Cursed letter share an address**: two StackKeys, never merge; Sort by address places them adjacent (flags are the last tiebreak) so the route still reads with the cursed one visibly separate.
- **If a stack holds units from different spawn shifts and is delivered after the older units' deadline**: per-mailId payment (R3), oldest first (R6) — the old units pay the late ratio, the rest pay full. The wire hint is Mail & Destinations R17's run list `(deadlineShift, count, unitSpawnValue)`, never `count × first`.
- **If `quick_move` has targets that reject the entry's category** (a log while only the mail-only truck is open): those targets are skipped in every pass; if *no* target admitted the category → `not_allowed` ("open something else"), else → `no_room` ("make space").
- **If any op targets hotbar slot 0**: `reserved_slot` ("Slot 1 is your hands"); AutoPlace, Grant and catches never select it, so only explicit placements can hit this.
- **If a client sends `rotated=true` for a square footprint**: the server canonicalises to `false` and accepts — the placement is geometrically identical, a reject would feel arbitrary.
- **If Bulk Mailer is drafted mid-run while the panel holds two 20-letter stacks for one address**: nothing moves (R15); the next merge, move-onto-stack, AutoPlace merge pass or sort consolidates them because `effMax` is evaluated at op time. `split k = 25` from a 20-stack is still `bad_count`.
- **If a def is edited smaller between runs** (chest 8×6 → 8×4; letter `maxStack` 20 → 10): in v1 this cannot reach a live container — defs are read at run start and there is no mid-run save. If resume is ever added, the loader must split over-full entries into `effMax` chunks (new ids, AutoPlace in place) and spill any entry whose footprint leaves the new grid into a spill bag (Intake overflow → backlog). Never silently clamp a count (R19).

### Lifecycle
- **If the open external container is destroyed or deconstructed while the player holds a cursor entry**: the external closes (`container_gone` thereafter, naming the destroyer from the tombstone table, R7), its remaining contents spill to a bag at its position, and the cursor entry is **not** in that bag — it stays on the cursor.
- **If a player is auto-closed at > 3.0 m mid-drag**: if the `pick` was already confirmed the server puts the entry away into the panel (R10; it stays on the cursor only if the panel is full, weighed by D1); if the `pick` arrives after the auto-close → `not_open` and the ghost rolls back. A `place` into the closed container → `not_open`.
- **If a player dies holding a cursor entry and has Second Wind**: the hotbar is kept; the cursor entry still goes into the bag (R13) — the cursor is a mid-interaction slot, not the hands; B ≥ 1 so a bag is created.
- **If a player disconnects with a `split` in flight**: FIFO. Disconnect-first: the split is rejected. Split-first: the piece lands on the cursor, then the disconnect handler AutoPlaces it, else spawns a WorldItem at the body (R11). After ch06 §6's 120 s the body spills a bag exactly like death (R11/R13; spec 06 §6 — former OQ3, closed).
- **If a thrown item lands on water**: it floats for 10 s where it fell (visible, not pickable, the red/white arc was the warning), then mail returns to the Intake **at the back of the outfeed queue** (or backlog) and non-mail is DESTROYED with `ItemsLost`. A bad throw into the sea is therefore never a free undo: the letter is out of play for 10 s plus the walk back, it re-enters behind everything already in the Intake, and its deadline keeps running. `drop` is already rejected while swimming.
- **If a thrown or dropped item lands on a vehicle roof**: WorldItems are never parented to vehicles in v1 (ch13 §4.5 carries players, not items); it is placed at the ground cell under the landing point; if that is water, the water rule applies (→ Throwing & Catching owns landing resolution).
- **If a thrown item lands on a belt**: units join the lane via per-unit `TryInsert` at that point, oldest first (ch13 §4.1, Throwing T6); the remainder becomes one WorldItem at the landing point, timer from landing, without blocking the belt.
- **If a `drop` spawn point is inside a mailbox volume**: nothing is delivered — mailbox volumes evaluate only FLIGHT entries. Drop is the "I don't want this" verb; a misdelivery from a careless drop would be unfair in a way a bad throw is not. If the spawn point is inside non-walkable geometry, the WorldItem spawns at the player's own position.
- **If a death bag expires while the Intake is full**: mail → backlog (never lost); non-mail → one Spill heap at the bag's position (R13); the bag entity is removed in the same tick.
- **If `worldItemCap` (512) is exceeded during a raid** (eight players throwing at goons ≈ 4 items/s): the oldest *mail* WorldItem **older than 10 s** (age ≥ 300 ticks, R12) is evicted first (lossless, → Intake, back of the queue); a letter that landed this second is never the candidate; a player's dropped axe from minutes earlier is destroyed only if no mail qualifies (R12). Belt leaks can create up to ~400 WorldItems in one tick, so eviction is bounded per tick by the number created and candidates come from per-class expiry FIFOs, not a scan; if all 400 are young mail the cap is exceeded until they age (R12).
- **If eight players die in one raid pulse**: eight Spill heaps (one WorldItem each) appear 30 s later, each wearing its owner's strap and hat icon; the cap is untouched (R12 counts WorldItems, not entries).
- **If `sort` fails on a hand-packed container** (a bike 8×2 packed by hand in a way first-fit cannot reproduce): `sort_failed`, container untouched, version unchanged, client shows "Can't tidy"; a valid hand-pack is never destroyed by a failed heuristic. **If `sort {address}` fails but size order fits**: the size layout is applied, reply `appliedMode: size`, toast "Tidied by size".
- **If players sort the Intake while a backlog exists**: the sort frees cells; the backlog drain (each tick after `Inputs.Apply`, Simulation Core phase 2) starts placing backlog mail NoMerge first-fit into the freed holes in the sort's own tick. Visible result: "sorted, then flooded with loose mail in the gaps" — correct, the backlog is older than anything and enters FIFO.
- **If mail would return to the Intake addressed to a district not yet unlocked**: cannot happen by construction (destinations are chosen from unlocked addresses; districts never re-lock). If bad data produces one, the Intake accepts it (R5 checks category only), Sort orders it by `districtId`, and it ages into dead letter.

### Degenerate strategies
- **If a player repeatedly drops and picks up an item to "reset" timers**: only the WorldItem's own 300 s expiry resets; mail deadlines are per mailId and per shift; the death bag's 30 s cannot be extended (emptying it into 300 s WorldItems is allowed, but non-mail then risks destruction). Nothing to exploit.
- **If a player throws unwanted (Cursed, far-district) mail into the sea to "return" it**: it comes back 10 s later at the back of the outfeed queue with its flags and deadline intact; walking to the Intake console and using "put anywhere" (R18) is faster and also goes to the back. Neither path launders anything.
- **If a player uses the deliver hold to farm**: it only delivers what matches, so it is exactly as fast as the mail you carry; a misdelivery needs no match at that door and mail selected on the hotbar, so the wrong-house gag still exists but costs one unit per hold, never a meter.
- **If a player uses death or deconstructing a loaded chest to move materials**: under the corrected R13 non-mail becomes Spill WorldItems *where the bag was* — position never moves, so there is no teleport.
- **If a player spams `sort` on the Intake**: each real sort ≈ 8 k probes (D3) plus one `replace` to the Intake's openers (non-openers get group counts only); the memoised idempotency rule makes repeats O(1). No cooldown (ch13 §4.3 removed rate limits); if profiling shows the pack hurts, `sortCooldownTicks` becomes a knob (default 0).
- **If a player abuses manifest "any free space" on the Intake** (hand-inserting unwanted mail): allowed — returning undeliverable mail is legitimate; it can only hurt the team's own Intake space, and the inserted entry gets a new `arrivalSeq`, so it leaves last. On the Depot it is a plain AutoPlace with merges, O(≤ 320) per op.
- **If a manifest `fromGroup` drop resolves against a group that changed** (another player consumed it): resolution is against current server state — empty → `stale_entry` with `byActorId`; smaller than `count` → partial `ok:true, requested 10, got 4, byActorId, cause: taken`, and the toast reads "Got 4 of 10 — Jules took 6" (H-FX-3).

## Game Feel

*Optional for a Core system; included because the panel is the player's second-most-used surface. From art-director.*

- **Responsiveness:** optimistic placement renders on the next rendered frame after input (target ≤ 16 ms; whether input precedes draw in the same engine frame is an engine detail to verify, not a design promise). The target confirm time is RTT + 1 tick (≈ 150 ms on a good home link); the ghost turns dashed only after `pendingDebounceMs` (250 ms, D7), so an ordinary connection never flickers and a slow one shows the honest signal, not a stall.
- **Animation never gates input:** squash → pop → hold (≈ 0.45 s) plays on the cell while the next op is already accepted; ten quick-moves take ten inputs, not 4.5 s. Animations are interruptible by the next state change of the same cell.
- **Sort** is one squash-pop-hold, never a cascade; the snap *is* the "Case Your Route" moment. Address text does not squash (art bible §7: address text never moves) — the cell does, the label re-renders in place.
- **Weight on the carrier (art-director ruling in Player Character Visual/Audio and P20):** hand pose follows the **held (selected) item's** class (§5: light aloft, medium hugged, heavy overhead); stride rate is proportional to real speed (no cadence thresholds); whole-body load (knee bend, lean, backpack sag) blends continuously on `min(P, 40) / 40` from `weightPoints`; strain face and hat flatten are reserved for throws. Slowness is read from silhouette and rhythm before the speed number.
- **The one unbreakable rule:** a grab the player loses is always *shown* losing — the ghost snaps back visibly, with the thunk, every time; never a silent revert. (Pillar 2; art bible principle 2; the M0 Fellowship criterion depends on contention being legible.)

## UI Requirements

*From ux-designer; visual language is locked in art bible §7. Phase tags: M-1a = hotbar + own panel; M0+ = external containers and contention; M1+ = manifest, sort, split, take-all.*

**Input contexts.** This GDD owns which contexts exist, which verbs live in each, and two rules: **one meaning per binding per context**, and **no binding carries both a tap verb and a hold verb for the same target in the same context** — Interact reaches several verbs only through target type (Input & Controls R4.2). ## Network

*Added 2026-10-16 (design review: network-programmer, godot-specialist). The systems index requires a Network section in every GDD that replicates state; this system owns the most. Vocabulary from spec 06 §4: **E** event, **R** request/validate/apply, **S** snapshot. Everything here is a design contract on the adapter; the wire format and the Godot 4.7.1 multiplayer API behaviour are NOT SOURCEABLE (OQ12) and the pipeline → becomes an ADR.*

**Adapter requirement.** This design requires **reliable, ordered delivery per channel** for inventory events, replies and snapshots, with payload fragmentation handled below the adapter. Container events, replies and per-container snapshots (subscribe, open, resync) travel **in-stream on one channel**, so no cross-channel ordering is ever assumed; channel 2 carries JoinState / RejoinState only. If the verified API cannot provide that on one channel, the version-gap resync path (below) becomes the normal path and the design must be revisited — verify before M0, not after.

**Bandwidth (PROPOSED — Networking & Session confirms).** Spec 06 §4.2 binds the *whole game* to ≤ 40 kbps down per client (≈ 167 B/tick at 30 Hz; 80 kbps ≈ 333 B/tick worst). Derived from what flows: an 8-player chest scrum (~5 ops/s × (16 B + 4 B per mailId) ≈ 24 B at 2-unit takes; a 20-stack ≈ 96 B) ≈ 32 B/tick, own-panel ops ≈ 4, raid WorldItems (~4/s × ~20 B) ≈ 3, Intake/Depot group counts at shift-5 mail flow (< 1 unit/s) ≈ 1. Hence **sustained ≤ 40 B/tick per peer** (10 s mean ≈ 9.6 kbps, 24 % of the client budget). **Bursts** — opening a container, a `replace`, a resync, a mid-Delivery RejoinState — are ≤ 12 KB each (a full loose Intake snapshot) and **paced at ≤ 800 B/tick**, so the largest lands in 15 ticks (0.5 s); one full burst per 10 s doubles the 10 s mean to 80 B/tick (48 % of 40 kbps, 24 % of the 80 kbps worst case). **PROPOSED reading (Networking confirms):** spec 06 §4.2's figures are 10 s means; a burst is a declared peak (≈ 192 kbps ≤ 0.5 s), ≤ one full burst per peer per `inventoryBurstIntervalSeconds` (5 s). Larger snapshots (a Depot of 20-stacks ≈ 30 KB) are paced the same way behind a "Loading" panel. Structural rules: non-openers of the Intake/Depot get group counts only; entry-level deltas per container per peer per tick are batched into one `ContainerDelta{version_from, version_to, ops[]}` and automation `TakeUnit`s coalesce into `UnitsRemoved{entryIds[]}`; `DeliveryOutcome` (Mail & Destinations R16) rides the same per-peer per-tick batch on Mail's byte share. **High-water valve:** if a peer's unsent inventory queue **excluding paced snapshot bytes** exceeds `inventoryHighWaterBytes` (24 KB), the server discards that peer's queued deltas (never replies, which still retire pending ops) for the container with the most queued bytes and marks it **resync-pending** for that peer; a fresh snapshot follows, paced as a burst — a slow peer costs latency, never unbounded memory or a desync. Measured by H-NET-7.

**Event and reply ordering (the rule that prevents double-apply and phantoms).** The client mirror is updated **only** from the event stream. Events a request caused carry its `reqId` to the requester; applying one removes that op from the pending replay (never rendered twice). A reply (`ok` / `ok:false, reason`) then **only finalises the pending entry** (reason, partial counts) and carries `resultVersion` per touched container (the version the op produced, or the current version on rejection). Events and replies travel on the **same** reliable-ordered channel, event first; the client therefore never sees a reply for a version it has not applied. Snapshots (subscribe, open, resync) are **version-tagged and sent in-stream** on that channel; every event, `ContainerDelta`, `GroupCounts` and `replace` carries `version_from..version_to`. Each container has one in-flight-snapshot flag per peer; while it is set — or while the client has **no mirror yet** for that container — the client **buffers** its events (bounded by `resyncBufferMax = 256` events; on overflow the buffer is cleared and the snapshot supersedes it — events are never applied out of order and never silently dropped), then discards those with `version_to ≤ snapshot.version` and applies the rest. A version gap (received `version_from = v + 2` after `v`) → request a resync for that container; resync requests are debounced (`resyncDebounceTicks = 15`). **Cross-container ops** emit one event per container, each with its own version; a viewer of only the source sees `Removed{entryId, count, mailIds}`, a viewer of only the target sees `Inserted{entryId, x, y, rotated, count, mailIds}` or `Merged{targetEntryId, addedMailIds}`; each side is validated by its own container's gap check only (the version *pair* is informational).

**Optimistic client.** Per subscribed container: confirmed mirror + version; a pending queue of `(reqId, op, dependsOn?)`; displayed state = mirror + replay of pending. Rules: (1) the pending queue is retired **only by a server reply** — a local decision that an op "no longer validates" is display-only and never un-sends anything; (2) temp entryIds for optimistic splits live in a per-connection namespace (`0x8000_0000 | slotIndex << 20 | counter`, `slotIndex` 0–7 = the connection's lobby slot), the server keeps the temp→real map per connection and resets it on rejoin, and an op naming a temp id whose creating op failed → `stale_entry` (a `deliver_matching` `selectedEntryId` included — it resolves by `dependsOn` or fails); (3) an op that depends on another's result carries `dependsOn: reqId` and is dropped locally (and answered `stale_entry` by the server) when that op fails — dependency is explicit, never inferred by re-validation; (4) `deliver`, `deliver_matching`, `throw`, `drop`, `take_all` and `sort` are **non-optimistic** (rendered pending, no local prediction); (5) **own sort**: while the client's own `sort` is pending, **every** op on that container (positional or naming an entry id, which consolidation may retire) is held locally, not sent, and released when the **sort reply** arrives — including `unchanged:true` and `sort_failed`, which emit no event. The client records `lastReplaceVersion` when the hold starts: entry-naming ops are always released; held **positional** ops (composed against the pre-sort layout) are released, with `baseVersion` = the mirror's `lastReplaceVersion`, only if no `replace` was applied in between (`unchanged:true`, or `sort_failed` with no foreign replace) — otherwise they are snap-backs (rule 6), never placed at a spot the player did not see, and never `stale_layout`; (6) a **foreign** `replace` marks every pending positional op for that container as doomed — shown as snap-backs at once, retired when their `stale_layout` reply arrives (rule 1 holds); (7) a version gap, or a reply missing for `pendingReplyTimeoutSeconds = 5`, → resync; timed-out entries **stay pending** until the resync snapshot is applied; the snapshot carries `ackedReqId` (the peer's last applied op) and only pending entries at or before it (u16 serial arithmetic) clear — later ones await their replies (Manual Delivery MD2 row 3); (8) `reqId` is uint16 per connection, wrapping (`pendingQueueMax` 16 keeps live ids distinct); the host (network adapter, keyed `(peer, reqId)`) keeps each connection's last 16 replies and answers a duplicate `reqId` still in that cache with the **original reply**, applying nothing; a `reqId` outside the cache is a new op (an op naming a gone entry → `stale_entry`, Throwing TC-E-5); cache and counter reset on rejoin. H-OPT-1/2/3 cover a happy chain, a failed dependency chain and the own-sort cases.

**Sort `replace`.** One `replace{version_from, version_to}` per container per tick to the container's entry-level subscribers, carrying the final layout `(entryId → x, y, rotated)` plus the consolidated entries' new ids and mailIds; the Sim raises one `replace` per sort call (Simulation Core R13) and the adapter merges one container's replaces of a tick at flush, ahead of that tick's replies; group-count subscribers receive only the changed counts. Budget: ≤ 10 KB for 320 entries (a burst) (≈ 12 B per unchanged entry + mailIds for re-minted ones). Sending only `Sorted{mode, hash}` and letting clients re-run D3 is deferred until cross-client determinism of the Sim is demonstrated (spec 06 §8 does not require it).

**Join and rejoin.** `JoinState` (spec 08 §3.9's "summaries" is made precise here; spec 08 still needs the correction) carries: the joiner's own containers and cursor (**owner-only block**), `po_intake` and `po_depot` **group counts** with versions (entry-level state follows only if the joiner opens them), `backlogUnits`, `worldSeq`, all WorldItems (`entries[]`, `expiresTick`, `ownerId`) and bags (entity, `ownerId`, entry count — contents on open); FLIGHT entries are Throwing's. `PlayerState` is split into a **public** part (`weightPoints`, held-item kind for stance) and an **owner-only** part (containers, cursor) — no peer ever receives another's panel. **Budget on the canonical packed encoding** (the adapter serialises this blob; the wire overhead is measured separately): ≤ 32 B per entry + 4 B per mailId + ≤ 12 B per group + ≤ 20 B value hint per mail entry in the joiner's own containers (Mail & Destinations R17); size = 32·entries + 4·mailIds + 12·groups + 20·ownMailEntries; worst case at the cap ≈ 71 KB (512 WorldItems of 20-unit stacks ≈ 57 KB, 640 Intake/Depot groups ≈ 8 KB, a full panel ≈ 5 KB), ≈ 98 KB with Bulk Mailer 40-stacks, more while the cap is exceeded or heaps are large — paced, never gated; H-X-5's fixture ≤ 12 KB is the gate. Sent on channel 2 in ≤ 64 KB chunks (spec 06 §5); in Prep it is unpaced, a mid-Delivery RejoinState is paced as a burst; the joiner may **not** send container ops until the last chunk is applied (`not_open` before that). **Rejoin within 120 s mid-Delivery** (spec 06 §6 allows it; join-in-progress proper is Prep-only): `RejoinState` = JoinState + the frozen body's panel; the open set, pending queue and `reqId` counter reset; ops queued on the dead connection are discarded; the server derives the sender from the transport peer id mapped to the account, **never** from a client-supplied `playerId`.

**Host.** The host's in-process client goes through the same loopback adapter, mirror and pending queue as a remote client, and its ops are enqueued at the same poll point in `Inputs.Apply`. **Host advantage, stated honestly:** the host wins any contest decided within a remote player's one-way latency (≈ 30–60 ms at home RTTs), not only true ties. Accepted under the friends-only anti-pillar; telemetry `contestedGrabWinsByHost` is tracked per session, target ≤ 1.5× the host's equal-latency share (1/n of contested grabs) in M1 sessions — above it, revisit with input-time tie-breaking.

**Validation.** Range (R10, pickup ≤ 2.5 m) and leash are consistency rules on Player Character F5's claimed-and-corroborated position; ownership checks do not exist (ch13 §4.3). Malformed ops → `bad_request` (R7), never an exception. Periodically (every 2 s per subscribed container, like spec 06's `LaneChecksum`) the server sends `ContainerChecksum{version, hash}` (staggered across containers; group-count subscribers get a checksum of the counts). The hash is deterministic and order-independent over the canonical entry encoding (never `GetHashCode`; encoding and function → ADR); the client compares it **only when `mirror.version == checksum.version`**, over the confirmed mirror with pending ops excluded; a mismatch triggers a resync (backed off on repeats) and a telemetry event — this automates H-R19-2's "no client shows an entry the server lacks".

**Network knobs:** `pendingQueueMax = 16` (further input is held, not dropped), `pendingReplyTimeoutSeconds = 5`, `resyncDebounceTicks = 15`, `resyncBufferMax = 256`, `containerChecksumSeconds = 2`, snapshot chunk 64 KB (spec 06), `inventoryEventBytesPerPeerPerTick` / `inventoryBurstBytes` / `inventoryHighWaterBytes` (Tuning Knobs), `inventoryBurstIntervalSeconds = 5` **PROPOSED**.

### Client optimistic view
- **H-OPT-1 [UNIT, shared fixture: A and B both have chest C open at v10 (E1 = 20 letters at (0,0), E2 = Medium at (2,0)); the server has already applied B's `move {Plank → (6,0)}` → v11]**

  - **b display** THEN A's client displays E2 at (2,0), Plank at (6,0), E1 = 15, cursor = 5 letters id 501, chest **v12**, no phantom entries, no resync; B's client shows the same chest state at v12.

GIVEN `unchanged:true` or `sort_failed` with no foreign `replace`, THEN both are released with `baseVersion = lastReplaceVersion` and the move is accepted. GIVEN B's sort replaced C during the hold (A's reply `unchanged:true`), THEN the move is a snap-back. GIVEN instead that **B** sorts C while A's move is already pending, THEN A's move shows as a snap-back at once and is retired by its `stale_layout` reply.
