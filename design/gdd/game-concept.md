## Elevator Pitch

> It's a co-op logistics rogue-lite where you and up to seven friends run a post office on a
> procedurally generated island — delivering mail by hand, by throwing it, and finally with
> belts, sorters and trucks — while defending it from raids.

## Core Fantasy

You are a crew of postal workers who treat a mundane job with absurd seriousness — and then build a
factory around it. Hand-delivering a letter, hurling a package across a street into the right
mailbox, and later watching a belt and sorter do the same thing for you are the same fantasy at
different scales.

The sources state the tone as "friendslop on the surface, factory depth underneath" (pillar 2) but
do not state a deeper player-facing promise. `INTENT UNKNOWN` — the wording above is inferred from
the pillars and the title.

---

## Unique Hook

Like Satisfactory (or PlateUp), **and also** your delivery fails, comically, because you threw the
parcel at a teammate's head — throwing is the signature chaos mechanic that touches delivery,
co-op and combat at once (spec 13 §1, §4.1).

### Key Dynamics (Emergent player behaviors)

Stated or directly implied by the sources:
- Players split roles (carriers, builders, defenders) and shout coordination over proximity voice.
- Players race to automate a route before the next raid, then take manual control of a machine or
  vehicle to beat the automation's speed (pillar 3).
- Players improvise throws to save a late parcel, accepting the misdelivery risk.

### Core Mechanics (Systems we build)

1. **Delivery with address matching and a grid inventory** — hand delivery, carry devices, throwing.
2. **Automation** — belts, splitters/mergers, Address Sorter, inserters, depots, Mail Truck with NPC driver.
3. **Run loop** — five shifts (Prep → Delivery → Raid → Payday → Draft), quota, complaint meter, shop, perk draft.
4. **Combat and defense** — waves of four enemy types plus mega variants against walls, gates, turrets.
5. **Co-op networking** — listen server, up to 8 players, server-authoritative economy.

## Core Loop

### Moment-to-Moment (30 seconds)
Pick mail from the intake, match its address to a destination, carry or throw it into the correct
mailbox — correct delivery pays immediately, a wrong one costs money.

### Short-Term (5–15 minutes)
One shift: **Prep** (build and stock) → **Delivery** (move mail to quota) → **Raid** (defend) →
**Payday** → **Draft** (choose a perk). Mail volume, quota and raid size scale with player count.

### Session-Level (30–120 minutes)
One Arcade run (five shifts, 20–30 minutes), ending in Victory or Run Over, followed by Postal Rank XP.

### Long-Term Progression
Postal Rank unlocks content and never raises player power inside a run (pillar 4). Seeds and
Postage Stamps make runs different. Free Play (open world, land/sea/space tech trees, optional
story) is the long-term intent; Arcade is v1.

### Retention Hooks
- **Curiosity**: unlock tracks, perk drafts, new seeds.
- **Investment**: Postal Rank and unlocks (meta-progression only).
- **Social**: friends playing together — the primary hook.
- **Mastery**: harder stamps; a better factory per seed.

## Game Pillars

See `design/gdd/game-pillars.md` for the full statement. Summary (from spec ch. 00):

1. **The mail must flow.**
2. **Friendslop surface, factory depth.**
3. **Hands beat machines, until they don't.**
4. **Short runs, long game.**
5. **Scales from 1 to 8.** (Solo must be winnable *and* worth playing — the M-1a tester doubted the solo loop carries itself; measured by the solo check at M0.)

### Anti-Pillars (What This Game Is NOT)

*Proposed from the v1 cut (spec 00 §Scope, spec 13); approved at the concept-writing step, to be confirmed by creative-director.*

- **NOT a crafting or power-management sim**: both are explicitly out of v1; the player's attention belongs on routing and defense, not upkeep.
- **NOT competitive or PvP**: quota is a team goal and money is team-shared.
- **NOT hardened multiplayer**: friends-only trust model — no anti-cheat, the host can kick (spec 13 §4.3).
- **NOT an open world in v1**: Free Play is the long-term intent; Arcade is v1.
- **NOT a power-fantasy meta game**: Postal Rank unlocks content, never makes a run easier (pillar 4).

## Visual Identity Anchor

> Authored by `/art-bible` on 2026-10-15; the full statement is Section 1 of `design/art/art-bible.md`, which governs where the two differ.

**One-line visual rule: "Every parcel is a toy; every mistake is a prop gag."** Everything the player touches is chunky, saturated and toy-like, and every delivery outcome — good or bad — plays out as a visible physical event in the world, never only as a HUD number.

**Supporting principles:**
1. **Address before ornament** (Pillar 1) — when decoration competes with an address or destination marker, choose the address; mailboxes and houses carry a colour-and-shape glyph readable from ~20 m, text secondary.
2. **Honest outcomes are physical** (Pillar 2) — when a delivery result is ambiguous, show it in the world, not only the HUD; the throw arc stays green / red / white and nothing softens a bad throw.
3. **Chunky silhouettes, flat state colour** (Pillars 5, 3) — when two designs have equal appeal, choose the one more distinct at 30 px; machine state on small emissive panels; players differ by hat first, tint second; an operated machine shows its driver.

**Tone:** full slapstick (owner decision 2026-10-15) — carriers earnest, the world's reactions, physics and UI slapstick; the reconciliation with "a silly job played straight" is flagged for creative-director in the art bible §1.
**Enemy fiction (closes the spec 13 §7 open question):** the raiders are a **rival courier company's hired goons** — people in a magenta-sashed uniform, not monsters; the Clash-of-Clans placeholder roster is re-dressed as Runner / Flyer Tosser / Pallet Jack / Rush Delivery.

## Risks and Open Questions

### Design Risks
- The fun of hand-delivering and throwing mail is unproven. The M-1a prototype (2026-10-15, one internal tester, solo) gave a preference signal only: throwing was "much more fun than hand delivery", but the tester also said the base game "isn't the most fun idea" on its own and that the fun will come from multiplayer chaos and planned additions. **The solo loop may not carry itself; the fun may be social, not mechanical.** This puts weight on the two-friend greybox run and the M0 Fellowship criterion (see `prototypes/m1a-delivery-throwing-concept/REPORT.md`) and on Pillar 5's solo floor.
- Balance targets are unvalidated: solo win rate 20–30% and 4-player 40–60% (spec 13 §6 M4).

### Technical Risks
- Steam relay and voice from C# is unconfirmed; a GDScript transport layer is the fallback (spec 13 §3).
- Duplication or loss of items under multiplayer contention (M0 gate).

### Market Risks
`INTENT UNKNOWN` — not stated in the sources. No commercial release is currently planned (free / friends-only).

### Scope Risks
- The full design is described as "a 5-person-year plan" (spec 13 §1); v1 is cut to fit a solo developer plus friends.
- The v1 netcode simplification must hold (client-authoritative movement, no rewind buffer).

### Open Questions
- **Title** — "Performative Mail" is the working title (spec 13 §7).
- **Art and tone** — resolved 2026-10-15 by `/art-bible` (rival courier company; full slapstick); see Visual Identity Anchor above.
- **Audio** — music direction and a bark list.
- **Platform and price** — Steam-only for v1 is assumed; the owner indicates a free / friends-only build.

## MVP Definition

**Core hypothesis**: delivering and throwing mail is fun enough that the owner and at least two
friends each want to play it again (spec 13 §6, M-1 gate). Split by the producer (PR-SCOPE) into
two gates: **M-1a** tests delivery and throwing alone; **M-1b** adds the belt and the raid once M-1a passes.
M-1a prototype status: built and played solo 2026-10-15; PROCEED accepted with CD-PLAYTEST concerns;
the two-friend run is still required.

**Required for M-1a (delivery + throwing)**:
1. Hand delivery with the address-match prompt and a minimal inventory.
2. Throwing, with delivery/misdelivery feedback.

**Required for M-1b (after M-1a passes)**:
3. One belt + one sorter + inserter feeding two mailboxes; one scripted Barbarian raid against a wooden wall.

**Explicitly NOT in MVP**: networking, procedural generation, the run loop and shop, voice,
everything in spec 13 §2.2.
