## The Pillars

### Pillar 1: The mail must flow
Every system exists to move addressed items from the Post Office to the right mailbox. Automation,
vehicles, and combat are all in service of throughput and accuracy.

*Design test (derived)*: debating a feature that makes combat more spectacular but does not protect
or move mail — choose the one that protects throughput.

### Pillar 2: Friendslop surface, factory depth
The game reads as a silly co-op delivery sim. Under it are open-ended logistics systems (sorting,
routing, transport networks) with real optimization space.

*Design test (derived)*: debating a polished, realistic mail simulation vs. a goofy mechanic like
throwing that still obeys the acceptance rules — choose the goofy one, and keep the rules honest
(a bad throw is never softened).

### Pillar 3: Hands beat machines, until they don't
Autonomous systems work without players, but a player operating a machine or vehicle is always
faster. Players should never feel that automation removed their reason to move.

*Design test (derived)*: debating whether an automated route should fully match manual speed —
choose slower automation and a manual-control bonus (e.g. NPC driver at reduced speed, faster when a player takes over).

*Clarification — **ratified by the user, 2026-10-16** (proposed by the Grid Inventory GDD at its CD-GDD-ALIGN gate; narrowed and ratified after the 2026-10-16 design review — grid-inventory.md Open Question 17)*: a manual action may be instant and strong (the inventory's Sort button) **if the machine's value is that it works unattended**, and **automation must never remove the player's reason to move**. Later GDDs — Belt Logistics first — read the speed test above through this lens: the response to "the manual path makes the machine pointless" is to raise the cost of being present (without loading that cost onto solo play, Pillar 5), never to weaken the manual action.

### Pillar 4: Short runs, long game
Arcade runs are 20–30 minutes and always end. Meta-progression, seeds, and modifiers make each run
different; nothing carried between runs makes a run easier by default.

*Design test (derived)*: debating a Postal Rank reward that boosts player power vs. one that unlocks
new content — choose the content unlock.

### Pillar 5: Scales from 1 to 8
Mail volume, quota, and raid size scale with player count sublinearly so a solo run is winnable and
an 8-player run is busy rather than trivial.

*Design test (derived)*: debating linear vs. sublinear quota scaling with player count — choose sublinear.

## Anti-Pillars (What This Game Is NOT)

*Proposed from the v1 cut; to be confirmed by creative-director.*

- **NOT a crafting or power-management sim**: both are explicitly out of v1; attention belongs on routing and defense, not upkeep.
- **NOT competitive or PvP**: quota is a team goal and money is team-shared.
- **NOT hardened multiplayer**: friends-only trust model; no anti-cheat, the host can kick (spec 13 §4.3).
- **NOT an open world in v1**: Free Play is the long-term intent; Arcade is v1.
- **NOT a power-fantasy meta game**: Postal Rank unlocks content, never makes a run easier.
